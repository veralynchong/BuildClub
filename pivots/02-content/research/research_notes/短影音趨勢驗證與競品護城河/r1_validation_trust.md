## Q1. Which signals actually indicate a trend / predict that copying a format will perform, and their pitfalls?

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed or preprint evidence I could find says the creator's own audience size is the strongest single predictor of a short video's virality, so raw views are confounded by account size and a per-account baseline (your outlier score) is justified in principle. I found NO rigorous study that validates "number of accounts posting a format in 14 days" as a predictor that copying will perform; treat that as an untested heuristic, not a validated signal.

### Cited Findings
- A 400-video labelled TikTok study found creator follower count was "the most powerful predictor" of virality; shot scale (close-up/medium), video lifespan, text presence and point of view also mattered. Small sample (n=400), 2021, preprint, no journal venue listed — [Slapping Cats, Bopping Heads, and Oreo Shakes (arXiv 2111.02452)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02452) [preprint; older than the 2024-2026 preference]
- Recent short-video popularity-prediction work uses video, audio, text AND metadata (such as author follower count) and four engagement targets (views, likes, comments, shares); accuracy reported as MAPE. These are INFORMS 2024 Data Mining Challenge entries, i.e. competition write-ups, not independent validation — [Multi-Modal Video Feature Extraction for Popularity Prediction (arXiv 2501.01422)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01422), [Multi-modal and Metadata Capture Model for Micro Video Popularity Prediction (arXiv 2502.17038)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.17038) [preprints; snippet only, accuracy figures not verified]
- For NEW videos with few interactions, Snapchat researchers (ECCV 2024) argued raw view counts are a poor target and proposed normalised metrics, namely normalised average watch percentage (NAWP) and engagement continuation rate (ECR), on a 90,000-video dataset — [Delving Deep into Engagement Prediction of Short Videos (arXiv 2410.00289)](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2410.00289) and [ECCV 2024 poster listing](https://eccv2024.ecva.net/virtual/2024/poster/1835) [snippet only; note retention-style metrics are not exposed by public APIs for third-party videos, see Q2]
- Hashtag diffusion research: a combined network + identity model reproduces hashtag cascades better than either alone, on 1,337 hashtags. This is Twitter data, not short video — [The Role of Network and Identity in the Diffusion of Hashtags (arXiv 2407.12771)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12771) [preprint; cross-platform generalisation unproven]
- Early detection of which cascades will become popular from limited early observation is feasible via survival analysis, tested on Twitter, Weibo and Digg, not on short video — [Detecting Popular Social Events through Limited Observation with Deep Survival Analysis (arXiv 2410.01320)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01320) [preprint; snippet only]
- Small-sample ranking pitfall (general statistics, classic): sorting by raw average lets 2-for-2 beat 100-of-101; ranking by the lower bound of the Wilson score interval keeps thinly-evidenced items from topping a list — [Evan Miller, How Not To Sort By Average Rating](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-sort-by-average-rating.html) [blog by a recognised practitioner; 2009, updated to 2016; applies to proportions, not to view-ratio]
- Survivorship bias in outlier tools: commercial "outlier" products (e.g. Virlo defines viral outliers as 50x+ views vs creator follower count) sell outliers for imitation; filtering on high performers excludes the format copies that flopped — [Virlo outliers feature page](https://virlo.ai/outliers), [Unmetric outlier detection launch](https://www.destinationcrm.com/Articles/CRM-News/CRM-Across-the-Wire/Unmetric-Launches-Outlier-Detection-for-Branded-Social-Content-133159.aspx) [vendor claims; the survivorship framing is the search summary's inference, not the vendors' statement]
- View-definition drift inflates comparisons across time: YouTube Shorts changed on 31 March 2025 so a view counts whenever a Short starts to play or replays, with no minimum watch time; the old metric became "engaged views" in Analytics Advanced Mode; monetisation still uses engaged views — [TechCrunch, YouTube is changing how YouTube Shorts views are counted (2025-03-26)](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted) [secondary, fetched]
- A second YouTube change: Google's Data API revision history (a Hindi-language copy) lists an August 2026 entry saying view counting will be made uniform across public formats. Third-party sources report the date as 27 August and that autoplay/hover previews now count; one Japanese article says 24 August; the YouTube videos.list doc I fetched says viewCount "starting August 24, 2026 will also count views when playback begins, including autoplay and hover-to-play" — [YouTube Data API revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/revision_history?hl=it), [YouTube Data API videos resource](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos), [SociaVault analysis](https://sociavault.com/blog/youtube-view-count-change-2026); date conflict 24 vs 27 Aug unresolved [primary doc says 24; third-party says 27]. The SociaVault claims (about 1.7x higher views, 41% drop in likes per view) are a vendor's own channel sample; treat as unverified.
- Benchmark denominators are not interchangeable: Socialinsider computes engagement rate per post as (likes + comments) ÷ followers, averages across posts (mean, not median), excludes accounts under 1,000 or over 100,000 followers "because they usually alter the data", and reports organic posts only. Its sample: 67.5M posts, 579k accounts. The page has internal date inconsistencies (says it covers through December 2026 while being a February 2026 post), so cite cautiously — [Socialinsider Instagram benchmark study](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-benchmark-study/) [vendor; fetched]
- Same vendor reported Reels engagement per post of about 0.50% (2024) and 0.52% (2025) and platform average 0.48% in 2025 (-24% YoY) — [Socialinsider Instagram benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/data-geeks/instagram_benchmarks_2025.pdf) [vendor; snippet only]
- Practitioner claim that biggest view surge occurs in days 1-5 of a TikTok post, from a "6 million posts" study — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/tiktok-video-performance-over-time) [vendor blog; underlying study not verified]

### Inferences
- Because follower count dominates virality (Slapping Cats, 2021), a creator-relative baseline like views ÷ account median is a defensible way to strip out audience size. The weak points are the baseline itself: the median of how many posts, over what window, and whether the account's own audience has grown. Show those inputs.
- Ratio scores explode when the denominator is small (account with tiny median). By analogy with Evan Miller's argument, shrinkage or a lower-bound estimate (or at least a minimum-baseline-sample rule such as "median of at least N posts") is the standard remedy. Evan Miller's method is for proportions, so adapting it to view ratios (e.g. bootstrap CI of the median) is my suggestion, not a sourced method.
- "Number of accounts posting a format in 14 days" measures adoption breadth, which is the closest analogue to hashtag-cascade research (network/identity diffusion), but the cited diffusion work is Twitter-based. Breadth of independent adopters is plausible evidence of a real trend vs one viral post, but it does not show copying will perform; there is no counterfactual in a list of successful copies. To test it you need adopters including failures (outcome of every copy, not only top ones).
- View counts are not stable across time or platforms (YouTube 2025 and 2026 redefinitions, Meta view_count mixing paid + organic). Any cross-period outlier comparison must store the counting-definition version and date of capture.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2024-2026 study found that tests share/save rate, early-velocity-to-eventual-views, or sound/hashtag growth as predictors of short-form format copy success. Only the 2021 TikTok study and competition-style preprints surfaced. Share/save as predictors: unfound.
- No study found on "creator copies a trending format; does it outperform?" (imitation efficacy) or on homogenisation of short-video formats. Suggested next searches: ICWSM/CSCW/CHI proceedings, "TikTok sound trend adoption".
- No independent evidence for regional differences in trend timing (TikTok Research API does expose region_code, see Q2) — unsearched in depth.
- Peer-reviewed evidence on view inflation / bot views for short video: not found.

## Q2. What do the Instagram, TikTok and YouTube APIs actually expose for third-party videos vs your own account?

### Takeaway
Third-party (other accounts') public metrics are available but thin: Instagram Business Discovery gives like, comment and view counts only for professional accounts, with view_count mixing paid + organic; YouTube gives views/likes/comments for any public video; TikTok's rich public-video fields (views, likes, comments, shares, favourites, hashtags, music ID, region) are restricted to qualifying non-profit researchers. Commercial agency tools therefore generally depend on scraping or partner arrangements for TikTok, which affects the "real data" claim. (The last point is my inference; see below.)

### Cited Findings
**Instagram**
- Business Discovery returns another account's media with `comments_count`, `like_count`, `view_count` and `id` in the documented example; view_count "includes both paid and organic metrics"; the target must be a Business or Creator (professional) account; age-gated accounts excluded; individual media objects cannot be fetched directly (fails with insufficient permissions), only via the nested request — [Meta, Business Discovery](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/business-discovery) [primary; fetched]
- Saves, shares and reach are not mentioned on that page for other accounts' media. Absence in docs is not proof of unavailability, but nothing indicates they are exposed — same source. Also required permissions listed in the reference: instagram_basic, instagram_manage_insights, pages_read_engagement — [Meta, IG User business_discovery reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/business_discovery) [snippet only]
- `media_url` is omitted for videos with copyrighted/licensed audio (including Instagram library audio), flagged videos, and reels with downloads turned off — [Meta, Business Discovery](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/business-discovery) [primary]
- Sample responses differ across doc versions for the same post (5837 vs 5841 likes) — [Meta docs search result](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/business_discovery) [illustrative only; snippet]

**TikTok**
- Research API codebook lists 22 public-video fields: ID, create time, username, region code, description, music ID, effect IDs, hashtags, mentions, voice-to-text, duration, video label, and like/comment/share/view/favourites counts, plus an `is_stem_verified` flag. Public videos by creators 18+ only; page says tools return all public videos "including some not eligible for For You feed recommendation". Codebook last updated 10 September 2026. Rate limits and metric-refresh lag are not stated on the page — [TikTok for Developers, Research API codebook](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/research-api-codebook) [primary; fetched]
- Eligibility: academic institutions in the US, EEA, UK, Canada or Switzerland; non-profit/independent research bodies in the EU (beta in some other countries); researchers must be "independent of commercial interests", disclose funding, show ethical review; use must be non-commercial/public-interest — [TikTok for Developers, Research API](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/research-api/) [primary; fetched]. A commercial agency tool therefore cannot use it.
- Display API `/v2/video/list/` returns the AUTHORISING user's own public videos; the documented example fields are `cover_image_url,id,title`, and the page does not list view/like/comment/share counts (they are in a separate Video Object reference I did not fetch) — [TikTok for Developers, video list](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/tiktok-api-v2-video-list) [primary; fetched, field list incomplete]
- Field parity note: an R client (traktok) documents video search output including view/comment/share/like counts, region, creation time, effect IDs, music ID, hashtags and voice-to-text; the search summary said it did not clearly find favourites_count in standard video-query output, whereas the official codebook lists Favorites_count — [traktok Research API vignette](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/traktok/vignettes/research-api.html) [third-party, snippet]; contradicted partly by [TikTok codebook](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/research-api-codebook)
- TikTok Business API for third-party video metrics: NOT checked (no source fetched).

**YouTube**
- `videos.list` statistics: `viewCount`, `likeCount`, `commentCount`, `favoriteCount` (always 0 since 2015); `dislikeCount` hidden since December 2021, returned only if request is authenticated by video owner; `fileDetails`, `processingDetails`, `suggestions` owner-only — [YouTube Data API, Videos resource](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos) [primary; fetched]
- viewCount semantic changes: Shorts March 2025 (above) and a further August 2026 change affecting public view counts (above) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted), [YouTube Data API Videos resource](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos)
- A GitHub CLI changelog claims the "engaged views" metric survives only in the Analytics API and that Data API viewCount and Analytics views will diverge — [faborsky/youtube-cli-app releases](https://github.com/faborsky/youtube-cli-app/releases) [low-quality third-party; unverified]

### Inferences
- The only first-party, commercially usable, per-video public metrics across the three platforms are: Meta (likes/comments/views for professional accounts only), YouTube (views/likes/comments for all public videos). For TikTok no commercial public-video API surfaced in my research. So a TikTok-covering product must say how it gets data (scraping, user-authorised connections, partner feed) and the customer's "real data" expectation should be matched by a disclosure of per-platform source and coverage. This is an inference from absence of evidence; check the TikTok Business/Commercial Content API and vendor approaches (another researcher is covering vendors).
- Share rate, save rate and watch-time/retention are the signals most often argued to matter, but they are not exposed for other people's videos on Instagram, and TikTok's `share_count` is only available via the research-restricted API. A product built on public data should therefore not claim to rank by saves/shares/retention; it can only use views, likes, comments (and shares where available), plus hashtag/sound/format metadata it derives itself.
- Meta's view_count mixes paid and organic, so a "paid-boosted" video could look like an organic outlier. There is no public flag to separate them; the UI should disclose this limitation.

### Gaps
- TikTok Business API / Commercial Content API / Creator Marketplace: not researched; unknown what, if anything, is exposed for third-party organic videos commercially.
- YouTube Data API quota cost per call: not found on the fetched page.
- Instagram rate limits for Business Discovery, and whether `media_type`/`timestamp`/`permalink` are returnable for other accounts' media: not verified (page did not list).
- Evidence on scraping coverage gaps (what share of posts third-party scrapers miss): not found.
- Meta Content Library and Instagram hashtag search limits: not researched.

## Q3. How do product analytics and social analytics tools build trust (sample size, CIs, freshness, methodology docs, drill-down, benchmarks, anomaly flags)?

### Takeaway
The mainstream pattern is to show the uncertainty and the data coverage right next to the number: interval bars and minimum-sample gates (PostHog, Amplitude), a per-report data-quality indicator stating the percentage of data used (GA4), and shaded expected-range bands with flagged anomalies (Amplitude). I verified fewer drill-down and freshness UI patterns than hoped; those are in Gaps. Note the research on whether showing uncertainty actually raises trust is mixed.

### Cited Findings
- PostHog experiment results display horizontal bars per metric: 95% confidence intervals (frequentist engine) or 95% credible intervals (Bayesian); clicking a bar opens a time-series chart showing how the interval evolved — [PostHog docs, Analyzing results](https://posthog.com/docs/experiments/analyzing-results.md) [primary; snippet only]
- PostHog frequentist engine requires at least 50 exposures per variant (funnel metrics need at least 5 conversions per variant) before showing results — [PostHog docs, Frequentist statistics](https://posthog.com/docs/experiments/statistics-frequentist) [primary; snippet only]
- PostHog trends offer smoothing via 7-day/28-day rolling average and scheduled subscriptions; the page I fetched does not mention person/event drill-down, sampling or anomaly alerts — [PostHog docs, Trends overview](https://posthog.com/docs/product-analytics/trends/overview) [primary; fetched; absence on this page only, other PostHog pages may cover it]
- Amplitude Experiment: significance = 1 − p-value, default 5% false-positive rate; confidence interval is the range for the treatment-vs-control difference, "not a probability"; if it contains zero there's insufficient evidence to call a winner. Results default to sequential testing while Funnel Analysis uses a two-tailed T-test, so p-values may differ between charts. T-test intervals/p-values are computed only after target sample size is reached; requires at least 30 samples and 5 conversions + 5 non-conversions per variant — [Amplitude docs, Experiment Analysis view](https://amplitude.com/docs/experiment/analysis-view), [Amplitude docs, T-test](https://amplitude.com/docs/api/content/feature-experiment/experiment-theory/analyze-with-t-test) [primary; snippet only]
- Amplitude Anomaly + Forecast: anomalies shown as orange markers outside a light-blue confidence band; forecasts as dashed lines; three modes (agile, robust, custom) with configurable confidence level and training window; default training windows differ between pages (120 vs 365 days) — [Amplitude docs, Anomaly + Forecast](https://amplitude.com/docs/en/analytics/anomaly-forecast) [primary; snippet only]. Insights alerts use 95/98/99% thresholds — [Amplitude docs, Insights](https://www.amplitude.com/docs/analytics/insights) [primary; snippet only]
- GA4 Data quality indicator beside report titles/cards/explorations has three states: unsampled ("This report is based on 100% of available data"), thresholding applied ("only display the data … when the data meets the minimum aggregation thresholds"), and heavily sampled explorations (example "8.88% of available data"; "a smaller sample size means that the data in this report is less accurate") — [Google, GA4 Data quality](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12856703?hl=en) [primary; fetched]. Icon colours differ between third-party blogs; not confirmed by Google's text, so don't quote colours.
- GA4 states data "appears in the Realtime report and then in your other reports shortly after" with links to data-freshness and SLA pages that I did not open — [Google, GA4 help](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9212670) [primary; fetched]
- Mixpanel publishes ingestion-delay incidents with scope (US/EU residency, duration) and states no data was lost, only delayed (e.g., July 2026 up to ~2h; October 2026 ~7.9h for a subset of projects) — [Mixpanel incident #5430 (Pulsetic mirror)](https://pulsetic.com/status/mixpanel/incidents/5430/), [Mixpanel ingestion delay (isdown mirror)](https://isdown.app/status/mixpanel/incidents/667213-temporary-data-ingestion-delay-for-us-projects) [third-party mirrors of the status page]. A community post (bot-generated, not the official SLA) describes a 24-hour latency policy for exports plus 24 more hours for late data, and ingestion-time properties such as `mp_processing_time_ms` — [Mixpanel community](https://community.mixpanel.com/x/ask-ai/msg_6eGlG4e3dTK8/i-would-like-to-understand-why-some-users-are-upda) [secondary; unverified against official docs]
- Social analytics: Socialinsider publishes sample sizes per follower tier, formulas, exclusions (accounts <1K and >100K followers) and organic-only scope in its benchmark write-up, but reports means only and does not describe collection method — [Socialinsider benchmark study](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-benchmark-study/) [vendor; fetched]. Benchmarks by Dash Social (H2 2025, H1 2026) also exist — [Dash Social Instagram benchmarks](https://pages.dashsocial.com/hubfs/social-media-benchmark/2026/H1/instagram.pdf) [vendor; not opened]
- Research on uncertainty display and trust is mixed: a thematic-map study found uncertainty visualisation generally reduced trust (more as uncertainty rose; low-uncertainty maps did not differ from none), while other scholars argue it signals transparency; Hullman's 2019 survey found authors reluctant to show uncertainty for fear of lessening trust; the specific Padilla et al. 2022 claim could not be confirmed — [The Noisy Work of Uncertainty Visualisation Research (arXiv 2411.10482)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.10482), [Northwestern coverage of election-forecast trust work](https://mccormick.northwestern.edu/news/articles/2024/07/building-informative-and-trustworthy-election-forecasts) [mixed; snippet only]

### Inferences
- Common pattern to copy: each headline number gets (a) its sample size ("based on N videos from M accounts"), (b) an interval or confidence label, (c) a gate that suppresses or greys out results below a minimum sample (PostHog 50 exposures, Amplitude 30 samples), and (d) a data-coverage statement (GA4's "% of available data"). Sample thresholds in those tools are for A/B tests; the specific numbers do not transfer to Trend Finder, but the "withhold the verdict until enough data" principle does.
- Amplitude's shaded band + orange marker pattern maps naturally onto an outlier chart: show the account's normal range of views (median and interquartile band) and plot the video outside it. This is my design suggestion rather than a sourced practice.
- Because showing uncertainty can lower trust if it looks like noise, present it as a plain label (e.g. "High evidence / Limited evidence") with the numbers one click away, and be conservative by default. That recommendation is an inference from mixed evidence.
- Data freshness: no tool documented here shows a per-chart "last updated" stamp in what I verified; the GA4 pattern is a coverage indicator, and Mixpanel's is a status page. For Trend Finder a per-record "captured at" timestamp is cheap and defends against the view-count drift described in Q1.

### Gaps
- No verified UI example found of drill-down from a chart to underlying raw events/persons (Mixpanel "view users", PostHog persons, Amplitude "microscope"). PostHog's trends overview page did not mention it. Needs targeted doc fetches.
- Methodology-doc patterns (e.g., Amplitude or Mixpanel statistical methodology pages as trust artefacts) were seen only via snippets; no evidence on whether users value them.
- Social analytics tools' own trust UX (e.g., Sprout, Hootsuite, Later, Socialinsider data-freshness labels, "estimated" badges): not researched; Hootsuite/Sprout/Later reports not opened.
- Anomaly flag false-positive rates and benchmarks-as-trust: no evidence found.
- Padilla et al. 2022 finding about uncertainty and trust: unconfirmed.

## Q4. What would a credible, explainable "why we recommend this video" panel contain?

### Takeaway
No source describes such a panel for short-video trend tools, so this section is design synthesis from the evidence above; every element below is tied to a sourced finding or flagged as my proposal. The core idea is to show the inputs, the sample sizes, the caveats and the missing metrics next to each recommendation.

### Cited Findings
(Evidence these elements rest on; see sections above for citations.)
- Per-creator baseline matters because follower count is the strongest virality predictor — [arXiv 2111.02452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02452)
- Small samples mislead rankings; confidence-bounded ranking fixes it — [Evan Miller](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-sort-by-average-rating.html)
- Tools gate results behind minimum sample sizes and display interval bars — [PostHog frequentist docs](https://posthog.com/docs/experiments/statistics-frequentist), [Amplitude T-test docs](https://amplitude.com/docs/api/content/feature-experiment/experiment-theory/analyze-with-t-test)
- Data-quality indicator expressing "% of available data" — [Google GA4 Data quality](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12856703?hl=en)
- Normal-range band with flagged anomalies — [Amplitude Anomaly + Forecast](https://amplitude.com/docs/en/analytics/anomaly-forecast)
- Metrics differ in definition and may mix paid and organic — [Meta Business Discovery](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/business-discovery), [TechCrunch on Shorts views](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)

### Inferences
Proposed panel contents (my design synthesis; each maps to evidence above):
1. **Outlier calculation, shown not asserted**: "412k views vs this account's median 18k (median of last N posts, window dates) = 22.9x". Show N and window; flag when N is below a minimum, or when the median is tiny (ratio unstable). Basis: creator-size confound; small-sample instability.
2. **Evidence strength label** (e.g. High/Medium/Limited) derived from baseline sample size, number of independent accounts in the format, and age of data, with the rule visible. Basis: PostHog/Amplitude gating; GA4 coverage indicator.
3. **Format adoption evidence**: list the accounts posting the format in the 14 days, with count, their follower tiers, and the outcome of each copy (including the ones that did NOT outperform), so the user can see the hit rate rather than only the winners. Basis: survivorship-bias concern; this is the single most important defence and is not currently offered by the outlier-tool vendors found.
4. **Account diversity check**: how many distinct accounts vs repeat posters, and whether adopters are clustered in one niche/region. Basis: diffusion literature (network/identity) plus Research API `region_code` as an example of regional metadata.
5. **Trajectory, not only a snapshot**: chart of views at each capture over time, with capture timestamps, so a user sees whether growth is still accelerating or flat; flag when the platform's counting rule changed during the window (YouTube 31 March 2025, August 2026). Basis: view-definition drift.
6. **Data provenance and freshness**: per-field source (official API / public page / estimated), "captured at" time, and an explicit list of metrics not available (saves, shares, retention for Instagram third-party posts; paid/organic split unknown for Instagram `view_count`). Basis: Meta docs.
7. **Caveat and anomaly flags**: e.g. possible paid boost, very small account, video older than X days, view count definition changed, single-account cluster. Basis: Meta view_count note; Amplitude anomaly pattern.
8. **Raw-source drill-down**: link to the original post, and a table of the underlying records (post URL, views, capture time) the score was computed from, exportable. Basis: transparency; no direct sourced example found (see Q3 Gap).
9. **Methodology link**: a short page stating formulas, windows, thresholds and known biases, plus a changelog of score definition. Basis: Socialinsider publishing formulas/exclusions; Amplitude's stats pages.
10. **Honest framing**: "This video outperformed its account's baseline and the format is being adopted by M accounts; this does not establish that copying it will perform." Basis: absence of validated predictive evidence (Q1 Gaps).
- Consider a lightweight self-validation: backtest "recommended" items against later outcomes of copies and publish the hit rate. This is my suggestion; no source describes a competitor doing it.

### Gaps
- No user research found on what agencies themselves find credible; this would need interviews with the target customers.
- No sourced example of an existing short-video trend tool with an explainability panel; competitor catalogue is another researcher's scope.
- Whether showing evidence labels increases trust in this specific audience is untested (see mixed uncertainty-visualisation evidence in Q3).
