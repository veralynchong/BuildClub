# YC-style product demo / launch videos: examples and structure

Date accessed for every source below: 2026-10-27 (today). Video metadata (upload date, duration, view count) was read from YouTube via yt-dlp on that date; view counts will drift.

Method note: link verification = YouTube oEmbed returned the expected title and channel for every video ID listed as "Verified". Visual structure (title cards, zooms, ending) comes from my own frame contact sheets (1 frame per 2-4 s, 240 px wide, from 360p downloads) of 8 videos; I did NOT listen to audio. Voiceover vs music claims are therefore either from a YouTube auto-caption file or from the transcript, or are marked "inferred". Web search was weak for this topic (mostly vendor SEO blogs), so examples were found by searching YouTube directly.

## Q1. Which YC companies / well-known startups have product videos in this style? (title, company, year, length, link)

### Takeaway
The "screen recording + auto zoom + keynote-style text cards + smooth cursor" style is the house style of design-led dev-tool launches (Cursor, Linear, Raycast, Notion, Lovable), usually 35-90 s with no face and no voiceover. The YC-batch launch videos I found are mostly the opposite or a hybrid: 60-120 s, founder or narrator voiceover, burned-in captions. Supabase and Cal.com: I found no 2023-2026 launch film in this style (gap).

## Q2. What structure do they follow, and typical length?

### Takeaway
Two dominant patterns. (a) Brand-film pattern (Cursor, Linear, Raycast, Notion, Lovable): 30-90 s, a title/logo card, 3-5 feature beats each introduced by a one-line text card and shown as a zoomed screen recording, then tagline + logo. (b) Founder/startup pattern (YC advice): about 60 s, with a setup of roughly 10-20% at the start, then demo, then ask.

### Cited Findings
- Cursor 2.0 (64 s): logo card "2.0" first, then benchmark chart, then "And a new Cursor built for agents.", then three feature cards each followed by UI recording — own frame review, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=An8IM-kPyms)
- Linear Agent (55 s): UI + typed prompt (about 0-35 s), result, generated spec/doc, diagram, zoom-out, then wordmark, tagline, logo at the end (about the last 8 s) — own frame review, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRql2VJ99gM)
- Raycast (39 s): five fast UI beats, two "NEW / ..." feature label cards, closing "NEW RAYCAST COMING 2026" card then logo — own frame review, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mi173xGb0ZA)
- Cardboard (61 s): live-action problem (about 0-17 s), "Introducing Cardboard" at about 20 s, product demo to about 50 s, logo + URL end card — own frame/transcript review, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wUshe13d2aw)
- Farza Majeed on YC's Startup School: "Most of these videos are one minute long ... for a video that's one minute long, you need a setup, but it should only be like 10, 20%." The first 10 seconds should cover "what you're doing, why it's important, let's get into it." — [YC Startup Library transcript](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/fY-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos); video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v12aSIZIXUs (published 2026-10-23, 47 min)
- His formula: "This is who I am. This is what's going on. This is what I'm working on. Let me show you. Everyone wants a story. And so how can a demo just be a mini story?" — same source
- He frames launches as continuous mini-demos on algorithmic platforms rather than one big launch: "you're not really making posts anymore. You're actually just constantly launching all the time" — same source
- "Beats, not cuts": his no-cuts video still has beats (hook "Hey, no hands", song plays then ducks under speech, then show the Stripe dashboard); "I'm always just trying to keep the story moving ... not trying to keep your attention" — same source
- YC chapter list for that talk (from video description): "Demos, not launches" 02:56, "The 'did my eyes stop' test" 08:34, "Scripting videos that sound unscripted" 09:58, "Beats, not cuts" 37:07, "The gear, and why audio matters most" 40:49 — [YouTube description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v12aSIZIXUs)
- Third-party blogs (marketing, low reliability, not verified against YC): Flowjam recommends 30-60 s founder-led with metrics early, demo in middle, one CTA at end; an "analysis of 250 launch videos" claims a 90-second sweet spot; these conflict with each other — [Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-launch-video-example-copy-this-script-2026), [Awesomic report](https://www.awesomic.com/launch-video-report) (both only seen as search snippets; not fetched)

### Inferences
- Observed lengths: Linear 33-55 s, Raycast 39 s, Arc 34 s, Cursor 64-90 s, Notion 90 s, Lovable 80 s, Cardboard 61 s. A 30-90 s window with 60 s as the YC-recommended center is well supported.
- Feature beats run about 5-10 s each (Cursor 2.0: roughly 4 feature cards in 64 s; Raycast: ~5 beats in 39 s).

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of hook retention was found from a primary source.
- I could not confirm any official YC guideline that fixes a launch video length; YC's own pages I read do not set one.

## Q3. Voiceover vs music-only with captions? Face on camera?

### Takeaway
The polished "keynote" examples (Cursor 2.0, Linear, Raycast, Notion, Lovable) are text-card + music, no face and no voiceover. The YC startup examples use a human voice: founder VO (Hey Clicky), narrator VO with burned-in captions (Cardboard), or a face on camera (YC advice).

### Cited Findings
- Notion 3.0's auto-caption file has only "[Music]", "[Applause]" and a couple of stray words, and its frames show caption cards ("You assign the tasks.") — own review of [caption file/frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R1cF4T4lgI4)
- Cardboard: narrated ("You've got hours or days of incredible footage...") with burned-in captions and background music cues — own review, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wUshe13d2aw)
- Hey Clicky: voiced by the founder with scripted but casual copy — [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=km54y7Xd_7g)
- YC (Farza): "Who is the person making this thing? ... Show your face very clearly" and "always start with a very clear, hi, this is what I'm working on" — [YC transcript](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/fY-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos)
- YC (Farza): "The most important thing in any video you make ... is audio quality. If your audio is bad, I'm bouncing." He gives a DJI mic and iPhone as the camera level; for screen demos "I always use ... Screen Studio ... it records your screen, records your local audio, makes your cursor look good. And I think it costs like 30 bucks." — same source
- Cursor 3 (2026) shifted to two people talking on camera in a room, intercut with UI — own frame review, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UxbULt_hCdA)
- YC's application video (a different thing) wants founders talking for 1 minute with "nothing except the founders talking" and "This is not the place to submit a demo or promotional video" — [YC: The Application Video](https://www.ycombinator.com/video)

### Inferences
- Cursor 2.0, Linear Agent, Raycast: no auto-captions were produced, which is consistent with music-only, but I did not listen; treat as inferred.
- Screen Studio is explicitly named by a YC-hosted speaker as the standard tool for cursor smoothing + auto zoom for founder demos, so it is a legitimate "YC-style" reference tool.

### Gaps
- Whether Hey Clicky's featured demo shows the founder's face (not verified).

## Q4. How does the first 5 seconds hook?

### Takeaway
Two hooks: (a) visual/typographic statement in the first card (Lovable "Less than 2% of the world ... but that's about to change", Arc's archival "1.0s" film, Cursor "2.0" logo); (b) immediate product in motion (Linear typing a prompt, Raycast search field close-up, Hey Clicky "Hey, today we're adding dictation..."). YC's advice is the first frame/sentence must stop a scroller.

### Cited Findings
- Cursor 2.0: opens on a logo + "2.0" card, benchmark chart follows within about 3-6 s — own frame review, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=An8IM-kPyms)
- Linear Agent: opens directly on the app and a camera drift into a prompt box — own frame review, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRql2VJ99gM)
- Raycast: opens on a large-scale search field "Search" then a "clip" query — own frame review, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mi173xGb0ZA)
- Lovable 2.0: opens with a text statement on a pastel gradient — own frame review, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xDwR1_vrIg8)
- Cardboard: opens on a problem scene (SD card into laptop) with "You've got hours or days of incredible footage" at 0:00 — [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wUshe13d2aw)
- YC (Farza): "Did my eyes stop?" test: ask whether you yourself would stop on the first sentence/frame. "Most people when they're scrolling see the first three seconds." His first frame was deliberately Xcode (to attract curious developers), with a purposeful zoom into a tiny UI element and moving it so the eye catches motion — [YC transcript](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/fY-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos)
- YC (Farza): study Reels for templates, e.g. a caption that starts "two months ago" so viewers wonder "two months ago what?" — same source

### Inferences
- A deliberate zoom into a small moving element in the first 3 s is both the YC advice and what auto-zoom tools (Screen Studio, Cursorful) produce, so "auto zoom" is functionally a hook device, not just polish.

### Gaps
- No measured data on which hook type retains best.

## Q5. How do they show features? How do they end (CTA)?

### Takeaway
One feature = one beat = one short text card (3-6 words) + a zoomed UI recording of the exact interaction, with a cursor or typed prompt visibly driving it. Endings in the polished films are tagline and logo, usually without a hard CTA; the startup/YC examples add a URL.

### Cited Findings
- Cursor 2.0: cards "Run a task across multiple models.", "Review changes in a single place.", "Work with a built-in browser" each followed by zoomed UI — own frame review
- Notion 3.0: cursor movement and typed prompts shown in a white UI frame, then "What can I wrangle up for ya?" prompts, caption cards between — own frame review, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R1cF4T4lgI4)
- Raycast: feature labelled with a tiny mono card "NEW / LAUNCHER DESIGN" — own frame review
- Endings: Linear = wordmark, "Linear. At your command.", logo; Raycast = "NEW RAYCAST COMING 2026" then logo; Cursor 2.0 = not clearly captured in my frames (sheet ended on UI); Arc = "Today" then blue card "arc.net"; Lovable = "Now it's your turn" then logo; Cardboard = logo, "usecardboard.com", "made with cardboard" — own frame review of each video linked in Q1
- YC Launch posts are text posts that link a demo video; a0.dev's post links its YouTube demo and uses "Check out our demo ..." — [Launch YC: a0.dev](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Mjx-a0-dev-build-mobile-apps-in-minutes)
- YC's Launch HN instructions: neutral factual title; readers should be able to try the product, with a demo or video as a fallback — [Launch HN Instructions](https://news.ycombinator.com/yli.html) (seen via search snippet; not fetched)
- YC's own advice on channels: Farza says the same video did ~3M views on Twitter and ~20k on YouTube, yet YouTube was "one of our highest converting videos"; publish to all platforms and see what converts — [YC transcript](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/fY-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos)

### Inferences
- For the BuildClub demo: a 45-60 s cut with 4 feature beats (text card, zoomed screen recording, cursor), then wordmark + one-line tagline + URL, matches both camps.

### Gaps
- No explicit CTA wording (e.g. "Try it free") was observed in the brand films; I did not check descriptions/pinned comments.

## Q6. What does YC itself advise about demo videos?

### Takeaway
YC's current (Oct 2026) advice, from the Startup School episode with Farza Majeed and Aaron Epstein, is: treat every demo as a launch; script it but make it sound off the cuff; about one minute; 10-20% setup; hook in 3 seconds; show your face; audio quality first; post continuously across platforms. Older YC advice warns against video in the live Demo Day pitch, and YC's application video explicitly excludes demos.

### Cited Findings
- "There's a misconception that a successful launch video needs to cost tens of thousands of dollars and feel like a high production commercial." (episode description) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v12aSIZIXUs)
- Farza: "a video where I ... say hi and I talk about my product is probably gonna perform way better than like a twenty thousand dollar produced video" — [YC Library](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/fY-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos)
- Scripting: "If I like reading it, then that means it'll probably sound good if I say it out loud" ; the script grounds you in front of the camera; "I'm the audience" — same source
- On imperfection: his Hey Clicky launch video was recorded "five minutes before dinner" and took off; "to not be cringe, you must first be cringe"; the team should ship it regardless of how it feels — same source
- Chapter "Why the first hire was a filmmaker" (39:01) indicates even this founder later hired a filmmaker — [description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v12aSIZIXUs)
- Demo Day pitch guide (Geoff Ralston, 2016-07-25): "demos seldom work in modern demo day presentations" and under Common Errors, "Including a video. They are almost never a good idea," because investors "want to see you perform live" — [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/) (old; about live pitches, not launch videos)
- YC application video: 1 minute, "nothing except the founders talking," no recited script, "not the place to submit a demo or promotional video" — [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/video)
- Kat Mañalac (YC Outreach) Startup School talk "The Best Way To Launch Your Startup" (2023-02-09, 21:08) covers one-sentence pitch and types of launches (chapters: "One Sentence Pitch" 03:42, "Types of Launches" 12:05); I could not retrieve its transcript, so I cannot cite its specific video advice — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u36A-YTxiOw)

### Inferences
- YC's modern stance is "authentic and frequent" over "polished and rare". The requested Linear/Cursor-style keynote film is a different, higher-production tradition. A hybrid (founder-style script + auto-zoom screen recording via Screen Studio-like tooling) fits both.

### Gaps
- No Michael Seibel or Garry Tan post specifically about demo/launch videos was found. Seibel's "How to Plan an MVP" ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hHMwLxN6EM)) is about MVPs, not videos, and I did not review it.
- I found no official "Launch YC video guidelines" page; https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/about returned 404.

## Q7. Common mistakes to avoid

### Takeaway
From YC and observed contrast: overproducing and waiting for a perfect launch, a slow setup, weak first frame, bad audio, and burying the product. Other items below are my inferences or vendor-blog claims.

### Cited Findings
- Treating launch as a one-time polished event; "you want a channel" you can repeat — [YC transcript](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/fY-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos)
- Not posting because it feels not good enough: "I thought it was horrible ... three million views on Twitter" — same source
- Bad audio: "If your audio is bad, I'm bouncing," even 720p is fine with crisp audio — same source
- Long setup: setup should be about 10-20% of a one-minute video — same source
- A Launch HN commenter on an onu launch criticised a demo for being too long with poor audio and suggested ~30 s with captions — [Launch HN: Onu](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36140916) (search snippet only, a single user comment)
- Overproduction can reduce trust if it feels inauthentic — [Vibrantsnap](https://www.vibrantsnap.com/blog/product-video-examples-what-works-best) (vendor blog; weak)
- Reciting a script in the application video is called out by YC (different context) — [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/video)

### Inferences
- Likely mistakes for the BuildClub video: starting on a logo or a long title rather than the product moving; zooming without a purpose (zoom should target the thing the card just named); feature cards longer than ~6 words; no end URL; music too loud over narration (Farza ducks music under speech).

### Gaps
- No systematic failure analysis from a primary source.
