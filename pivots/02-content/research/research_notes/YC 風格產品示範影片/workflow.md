# Production workflow for a <90 s "YC launch" style SaaS demo video (screen recording + title cards, no people on camera)

Date accessed for all sources: 2026-10-27. "Inference" = my reasoning, not a sourced fact. Many sources are vendor marketing; flagged where relevant. No Reddit threads were actually retrieved (search returned vendor blogs / Indie Hackers only); see Gaps.

## 1. Word count, shot count, and title-card hold time for 60-90 seconds

### Takeaway
Narration budget for 90 s is roughly 140-225 words if the voice never stops, and less (about 110-170) once you leave breathing room for title cards and silent demo beats. Sourced length guidance for startup demos clusters at 60-90 s; no source gives a firm shot count or a title-card duration, so those are inferences from reading-speed rules of thumb.

### Cited Findings
- About 140-150 words of narration per minute; recommended demo length two minutes or less — [Consensus, "product video scripts get better with 3 constraints" (Sept 23, 2025)](https://goconsensus.com/blog/product-video-scripts-get-better-with-3-constraints)
- A startup-focused producer suggests a 90-second demo script of about 240 words or fewer, with a problem / solution / call-to-action structure — [Clarity.fm Q&A](https://clarity.fm/questions/1931) (search snippet only; page not fetched, date unknown)
- Faster "moderate" speech is about 3 words/second (~180 wpm) per a script-timer tool — [DHL video script timer](https://video.dhl.com/video-script-timer.html) (snippet only)
- Landing-page demo 60-90 s; outbound/nurture 30-45 s; paid-social cutdowns 6-15 s; record the full demo once and cut shorter versions from the same take — [Stackmatix, "Product Demo Video for Startups" (Sept 4, 2026)](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/product-demo-video-for-startups)
- Stackmatix: open with a recognizable problem within the first 5 s, show one job completed in the real UI, end on the outcome rather than a logo; record the real product (not animation) at pre-seed/seed — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/product-demo-video-for-startups)
- Studio-claimed: 12 of the top 15 launch videos in its own data run 30-90 s; "show the product early, no logo card, no manifesto"; first 15 s decide whether viewers watch to the end (cited to a third party). Author is a video studio selling these services and the data is internal/unverified — [Flowjam, "YC Launch Video Example" (2026)](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-launch-video-example-copy-this-script-2026)
- A 2018 YC-alum guide: attention span for a demo/explainer is 60 s, 90 s at most; short intro, feature walkthroughs, brief outro repeating the tagline; "don't worry if it isn't polished" — surfaced via search snippet of a third-party blog (not fetched; the snippet did not name the exact URL, so treat as weakly sourced)
- Wistia 2026 State of Video analysis (13M+ videos): videos under 1 minute average ~52% engagement; product demos are filed under the 1-5 minute band; most viewers drop off around the halfway point — [Wistia, "How to Choose the Right Marketing Video Length" (Jul 1, 2025)](https://wistia.com/blog/optimal-video-length)
- Tella: marketing demos about one minute at most; feature demos five minutes at most — [Tella blog (Jun 21, 2023, older than preferred range)](https://www.tella.com/blog/how-to-create-a-killer-product-demo-video)
- Arcade (interactive step-by-step demos, not video, so only analogous): 14M sessions; completion 22% for 1-5 steps, 40% for 9-12 steps, trough at 16-20 steps; users who pass step 7 are 2.3x more likely to finish; deliver value within the first six steps; hotspot copy under 60 characters (completion drops 12% above); only "1-3 short bursts of video" recommended — [Arcade 2026 Benchmarks](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks). Internal inconsistency: the same article also says video can be up to half of content without hurting completion.
- Supademo: marketing demos about 8-12 steps; record at the same dimensions every time — [Supademo recording best practices](https://supademo.com/academy/create-your-demo/recording-best-practices) (page undated)
- On-screen text timing rules of thumb: about 1 second per 2-3 words (average adult reads ~4.5 words/s); hold long enough to read twice; slow readers about 200 wpm — [Biteable word-count article](https://biteable.com/blog/video-word-count/) and [SSW rule on reading time](https://www.ssw.com.au/rules/post-production-do-you-give-enough-time-to-read-texts-in-your-videos) (snippets only; "none is an official standard")
- Pitch-deck-slide pacing for on-stage 60 s Demo Day style: about 10 s per slide, one idea per slide, ~60 pt text, one number per slide; vendor-asserted, not verified as YC rule — [Flowjam, "YC Style Launch Video: Demo Day Ready"](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-style-launch-video-demo-day-ready-2026) (page dates conflict: Oct 2025 / Jul 2026 / Oct 2026)

### Inferences
- A workable 75-90 s budget: about 8-10 shots total = 4-5 title cards plus 4-6 app clips (Arcade's 9-12-step sweet spot and Supademo's 8-12 are interactive-demo figures, used here only as a loose analogy).
- Suggested timeline (my construction): hook card 3-4 s; problem card 3-4 s; 4-5 app clips of 8-12 s each (about 45 s total); one "result / proof" card 4 s; closing card with name + URL 4-5 s. Total about 70-85 s.
- Title-card hold: reading-rule math gives 3-6 words = 2-3 s, 8-10 words = 3-4 s, plus about 0.5 s for the transition; never hold a card longer than about 5 s in a 90 s video.
- Word budget: at 140-150 wpm, 80 s of actual narration is about 190-200 words, but title cards usually carry their own text and clips often run silent or with one-line voiceover; target 110-170 spoken words and write it, then time it aloud.
- Cut-down logic from Stackmatix (record once, cut shorter versions) fits a two-person team: build the 90 s master, then derive a 30-45 s version and a 10-15 s clip for the pitch deck.

### Gaps
- No source gives a firm shot count or title-card duration for video demos; the figures above are inferences.
- No independent evidence that "YC launch videos" have a canonical 60 or 90 s length; all YC-specific length claims found come from video-production vendors (Flowjam etc.) and are unverified. I did not find a ycombinator.com page prescribing demo-video length.
- The Clarity.fm 240-word figure and the 2018 YC-alum guide come from search snippets only.

## 2. Best practices from vendor tutorials (Screen Studio, Tella, Arcade, Wistia, Vidyard) and creators

### Takeaway
Vendor consensus: one job per video, real product on screen, show the result early, short sentences, zoom to clarify rather than decorate, treat automatic zoom/cursor smoothing as a first pass to adjust against the narration, and pause after key changes so viewers can see the outcome. Vidyard-specific length guidance was not found in retrievable form.

### Cited Findings
- Screen Studio records the screen without the cursor and re-adds a smoothed cursor in editing; cursor size/style can be changed after recording; auto-zoom follows click positions and does nothing where there is no click (add a manual zoom there); zooms can be dragged on the timeline; instant (no-animation) zoom exists; trim, cut, speed up; export up to 4K 60 fps; macOS only — [Screen Studio product-demo page](https://screen.studio/create/product-demo-videos), [Auto zoom guide](https://screen.studio/guide/auto-zoom) (via search summary), [Cursor guide](https://screen.studio/guide/cursor)
- Screen Studio cursor options: "hide cursor if not moving", "remove cursor shakes", "always use default system cursor" (consistency while selecting text), "stop cursor movement at end of video", "loop cursor position"; it also suggests hiding the cursor entirely for presentation-style content — [Screen Studio cursor guide](https://screen.studio/guide/cursor)
- Third-party Screen Studio review guidance: use zoom to clarify, not to decorate; a zoom on a button without showing the resulting screen is decoration; start with auto-zooms then adjust so each zoom lines up with narration; keep the demo to one task, no unrelated tabs/notifications/live customer data; add arrows/labels only where viewers might miss an interaction — [NemoVideo, "Screen Studio Review 2026"](https://www.nemovideo.com/blog/ai-video-workflows/screen-studio-review) (third-party, snippet-level)
- Tella: use plain language, avoid long sentences and jargon, essay-like intro / how-it-works / conclusion, include a call to action, record multiple clips and assemble them, add zoom and mouse-follow to emphasize details, use a clear voiceover (poor sound undermines trust) — [Tella blog (Jun 21, 2023)](https://www.tella.com/blog/how-to-create-a-killer-product-demo-video)
- Loom-style pacing advice: "Pause briefly after important changes so viewers can see the result"; do not click ahead before the page finishes responding; stop once the promised outcome is visible; watch the full recording before sharing — [MacMyths, "How to record your website for a demo" (Sept 30, 2026)](https://macmyths.com/how-to-record-your-website-for-a-demo/)
- Arcade: show value early (steps 1-6 are the decision steps); questions in labels raised click-through nearly 40%; AI-generated voiceover correlated with 14% higher completion (correlation only; Arcade's own interactive-demo data) — [Arcade 2026 Benchmarks](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks)
- Wistia: pacing and content density affect retention independent of length (via secondary summary); most viewers drop at around halfway — [Ngram summary citing Wistia](https://www.ngram.com/blog/demo-video-length) (secondary), [Wistia](https://wistia.com/blog/optimal-video-length)
- Vidyard: the retrievable material is general (screen capture plus selfie cam is the popular format; use a brief demo on the homepage to qualify prospects; speak directly to the viewer; don't worry about small slips) — [Spitfire summary of Vidyard](https://blog.spitfireinbound.com/curated-how-to-make-demo-videos-that-drive-pipeline-and-revenue-with-vidyard), [Vested Marketing](https://www.vested.marketing/blog/best-practices-for-vidyard) (snippets only)
- Solo-founder write-up pitfalls: waiting for perfection, "explaining instead of showing", opening too slowly; vendor guides add: static screen (use highlight/zoom), audio problems (background noise, poor mic placement, background music) — [VibrantSnap solo-founder guide](https://www.vibrantsnap.com/blog/solo-founder-guide-to-product-demos) and search summary (vendor blogs)
- Flowjam/Firecrawl-style launch videos: show install command and first useful output for dev tools; real footage end-to-end, not abstract motion — [Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-launch-video-example-copy-this-script-2026) (vendor self-promo)

### Inferences
- The "YC launch style" visible in public examples is better characterized as: first 5-10 s show the product or a one-line value statement, raw-but-clean screen footage, plain narration, and a single ask. This is inferred from vendor blogs; not confirmed from YC.
- Apply Arcade's step data cautiously: it measures click-through demos, not linear video. The transferable idea is "value before step 7" and "avoid the middle-length trough."

### Gaps
- Vidyard's own length recommendation (the Salesforce interview with its CEO) was not retrievable.
- Retrieved Tella material is a 2023 post; no 2024-2026 Tella guide with concrete settings found.
- No real Reddit r/SaaS or r/startups thread was retrieved; the search returned vendor blogs and Indie Hackers posts, so community-sourced "real" founder feedback is a gap (the `reddit-x-research` skill could close it).

## 3. Scripting and preparing the app for recording

### Takeaway
Sourced prep advice is consistent but generic: demo account with synthetic data, start on the exact first screen, close unrelated tabs, hide bookmarks, turn off notifications, record at the same dimensions every time, record in short separate clips. Specific resolution/window-size numbers were not found in a reliable source and are given only as inferences.

### Cited Findings
- Hide the bookmarks bar, turn off notifications, keep the desktop clean, record at the same dimensions every time (mismatched sizes break layout alignment) — [Supademo recording best practices](https://supademo.com/academy/create-your-demo/recording-best-practices)
- Close unrelated tabs and hide notifications; sign into a prepared demo account and start at the exact first screen; use sample data, not real customer data; wait for content to load before narrating; keep the pointer clear of text — [MacMyths (Sept 30, 2026)](https://macmyths.com/how-to-record-your-website-for-a-demo/)
- Use a demo account with synthetic/sample data; a separate Chrome profile is simpler than reinstalling; Demosafe is a Chrome extension that blurs/hides on-screen elements — search summary of [Demosafe](https://chromeboard.com/extension/demosafe-lflfcoefpbcphhhcdpjlkgjifbjbpaeb) (snippet-level; not verified)
- Record each clip as a separate take and assemble later (Tella: "record as many clips as you need") — [Tella blog](https://www.tella.com/blog/how-to-create-a-killer-product-demo-video)
- Screen Studio can export vertical and adjust zooms automatically — [Screen Studio via search summary](https://screen.studio/)
- Script consistency: pick a point of view (first/second/third person) and a repeatable structure; limit word count — [Consensus](https://goconsensus.com/blog/product-video-scripts-get-better-with-3-constraints)

### Inferences (practical checklist, my construction)
1. Script first: one-sentence promise, the single job to show, 4-6 app steps, one CTA. Write the title-card text and the voiceover together so the cards do not repeat the voiceover word-for-word.
2. Seed the app with believable fake data (names, brands, numbers that are round enough to read); reset DB to the same state before each take.
3. Fresh Chrome profile with no extensions, bookmarks bar hidden (Cmd+Shift+B), macOS Do Not Disturb / Focus on, Dock hidden, desktop icons hidden, battery/menu bar clutter hidden, dark/light mode matched to the deck.
4. Window/resolution: record a fixed 16:9 area (1920x1080 or a 2x retina capture downscaled to 1080p) and set browser zoom to 125-150% so text reads at 1080p/phone size (inferred; no sourced number).
5. Record each step as its own clip (3-5 takes per step is normal), moving the cursor slowly and deliberately, pausing 1-2 s after every important change (the pause advice is sourced to MacMyths; the 1-2 s number is mine).
6. Keep a take log and re-record rather than fix in edit when a click misses.

### Gaps
- No reliable source for ideal window size/resolution/browser zoom; the exact values above are inferences.
- I did not find a source that specifically recommends recording a Retina 2x capture versus native 1080p.

## 4. Recording and editing: cursor, zooms, speed-ups, transitions between app footage and title cards

### Takeaway
Use Screen Studio-style post-added smooth cursor and click-driven zooms, but review every automatic zoom so it starts just before the action and ends after the result is visible; speed up only dead time (loading, typing) and never the cursor itself. No source gave a numeric zoom-duration or transition-length standard.

### Cited Findings
- Screen Studio's smoothing turns shaky cursor paths into gliding motion and applies natural blur to fast moves; speed-up and trim are built in; zooms are draggable on the timeline — [Screen Studio](https://screen.studio/create/product-demo-videos)
- Start with automatic zooms and then retime so each lines up with narration — [NemoVideo review](https://www.nemovideo.com/blog/ai-video-workflows/screen-studio-review)
- Zoom to clarify, not decorate; a zoom must show the result of the action — same source
- Arcade-style benchmark: AI voiceover correlated with higher completion; keep labels short (under 60 characters) — [Arcade 2026](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks)
- Tella lists zoom and mouse-follow effects, custom backgrounds and layouts, subtitles as built-in editing options — [Tella blog](https://www.tella.com/blog/how-to-create-a-killer-product-demo-video)
- Pace: "stop once the promised outcome is visible" — [MacMyths](https://macmyths.com/how-to-record-your-website-for-a-demo/)

### Inferences
- Edit rhythm: cut on action; hard cut or 8-12 frame cross-dissolve between a title card and app clip, no fancy wipes (inferred; no source).
- Speed-ups: 2-4x for loading spinners or typing, with a small "2x" label so the viewer is not misled; keep the cursor path in normal-speed segments (sourced concern: sped-up cursor listed as a pitfall in the brief, but I found no source that quantifies it).
- Zoom timing: begin the zoom 0.3-0.5 s before the click, hold until the result appears, ease out; max about 2 zooms per 10 s clip.
- Title card to app transition: end the card on a frame whose background colour/layout matches the app window (see section 6) so a hard cut feels continuous.

### Gaps
- No source on numeric zoom duration/easing or transition length. Open-source Screen Studio alternative (Open Screen) exists per [Gigazine (2026-04-17)](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260417-open-screen) but I did not evaluate it; useful if the team lacks a Mac license, unverified.

## 5. Audio: voiceover, AI voice vs own voice, music, loudness

### Takeaway
Calm, clear, short-sentence narration is the sourced standard; AI voice is acceptable and correlates with better completion in Arcade's data, but no head-to-head trust/conversion comparison was found. Keep music low (practitioner rule: 18-25 dB under the voice) or skip it.

### Cited Findings
- AI voiceover correlated with 14% higher completion in Arcade's data (correlation, interactive demos) — [Arcade 2026](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks)
- Use a "calm voiceover" over a clean recording; keep narration minimal and let the product carry the message — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/product-demo-video-for-startups)
- Poor audio undermines trust; background noise, bad mic placement, and loud music are the common problems — [Tella](https://www.tella.com/blog/how-to-create-a-killer-product-demo-video); vendor guides in the search summary of the VibrantSnap/production guides
- ElevenLabs-type AI voice: script quality matters most; shorten long sentences, use contractions, one idea per sentence, read aloud once before generating; cloning your own voice needs a clean 1-5 minute sample and a paid plan (free tier capped at 5,000 characters/month) — [Subclip guide](https://subclip.app/blogs/how-to-use-elevenlabs-master-this-ai-voice-generator), [Tom's Guide](https://tomsguide.com/how-to/elevenlabs-now-lets-you-use-ai-to-change-your-voice-heres-how-to-sound-like-a-different-person) (search summaries; plan limits may have changed)
- Loudness: YouTube normalizes around -14 LUFS integrated with about -1 dB true peak as a safe ceiling (practitioner sources; some users land at -16 to -18 to avoid clipping) — [Wikipedia, Audio normalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_normalization), [Youlean](https://youlean.co/?p=30813) (search summaries)
- Music under narration: about -18 to -25 dB under voice (vidpros); 18-24 dB quieter than voice (another blog); narrator peaks around -12 to -6 dB — [Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/fix-background-music-too-loud-video/), [Overseeros](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/background-music-volume-under-voiceover) (practitioner advice, not standards)
- YouTube Audio Library: copyright-safe tracks won't be claimed via Content ID; some tracks are Creative Commons and need an attribution line in the description (filter for "no attribution required" to avoid); YouTube says it cannot give legal advice — [YouTube Help, Audio Library](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882)
- Pixabay Music listed as free with no attribution required — [vidIQ guide (2026)](https://vidiq.com/ru/blog/post/how-to-best-free-music-youtube-royalty-free) (secondary; verify the license page before use)

### Inferences
- Two-person team: record your own voice with a phone/earbuds mic in a closet or under a duvet, 15-20 cm from mouth, reading at about 140 wpm; record each clip's line separately so lines can be re-timed to clips. Or use AI voice for consistency; decide by listening test with 3 people (no evidence either way on trust).
- Since the video must also work silent on stage (section 7), keep music minimal or absent, and make captions carry the meaning.
- Keep the first export at -16 to -14 LUFS, true peak -1 dB; check on laptop speakers and phone.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head study on AI voice vs human voice for product-demo trust or conversion.
- Music licensing nuance (Pixabay license changes, Content ID false claims) not verified at the primary source.

## 6. Making the video and the pitch deck share one visual style (keynote-style cards + screen recording)

### Takeaway
No retrievable primary source describes how teams unify a deck and a demo video; vendor material suggests the same slide template can feed both. The approach below is mostly inference.

### Cited Findings
- A YC-style Demo Day pitch is described as about six slides at roughly 10 s each, one idea per slide, large legible type (about 60 pt), black on white, one number per slide; a 10-15 s pre-recorded demo clip is placed on slide 1 or 2 instead of a live demo — [Flowjam, YC Style Launch Video](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-style-launch-video-demo-day-ready-2026) (vendor; the attribution of these rules to YC's Startup Library was not verified)
- Screen Studio and Tella both offer custom backgrounds/layouts around the app footage, so the recording can sit on the same background colour as the slides — [Screen Studio](https://screen.studio/create/product-demo-videos), [Tella](https://www.tella.com/blog/how-to-create-a-killer-product-demo-video)

### Inferences
- Build the title cards as slides in the same Keynote/Figma/Slides master as the pitch deck, same font, palette, margins, and logo position, and export them as 1920x1080 PNGs (or a PDF) for the video editor so every title card is literally a deck slide.
- Put the app clip inside a rounded-corner window with the deck's background colour and drop shadow, so cutting between card and clip feels continuous.
- The reverse also works: take 2-3 stills from the app recording for the deck's product slides, so deck and video look the same.
- Keep text on cards to at most 8-10 words and at least the legibility rule above (about 60 pt on a 1080p frame).

### Gaps
- No source describing a team's actual workflow for sharing a visual system between deck and video; no verified example with source files.

## 7. Making a version that works as a live on-stage demo fallback (silent, captioned)

### Takeaway
Recording a backup demo is standard advice; silent-friendly burned-in captions are well supported. No source specifically covers a muted looping stage fallback, so that configuration is an inference.

### Cited Findings
- Record a backup demo video you can share if the live session fails; test internet, mic, and screen sharing before the demo — [Arcade, "Live demos guide" (Jun 23, 2025)](https://www.arcade.software/post/live-demos-guide)
- Backup-video advice for hackathon/pitch contexts: record a backup before presenting; have someone else drive the demo while the presenter narrates; internet outages and quota limits have broken real team demos — [Reskilll hackathon guide (2026)](https://blogs.reskilll.com/hackathon-demo-presentation-tips-pitch-3-minutes-win-2026/), [UNT software engineering workbook, ch. 11](https://openbooks.library.unt.edu/seworkbook/chapter/chapter-11-demos) (snippet-level)
- Burned-in ("open") captions are drawn into the frames, show on every platform, and cannot be turned off; many platforms ignore sidecar subtitle files — [AutoCaption blog](https://autocaption.io/blog/open-captions-vs-closed-captions), [Auphonic (2026-07-29)](https://auphonic.com/blog/2026/07/29/burn-in-subtitles/) (vendor blogs)
- Silent viewing is common: publishers reported up to 85% of Facebook video views occurred with sound off in 2016; this was self-reported by publishers, not an independent study, and is old — [Digiday (2016)](https://www.digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/)
- Rehearse the pitch so it is deliverable even without the projector — [Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-style-launch-video-demo-day-ready-2026) (vendor)

### Inferences
- Export two files from one project: (A) YouTube master with voiceover plus captions; (B) "stage" version with NO audio dependence: burned-in captions or short on-screen labels, no music or very quiet, 1080p H.264 MP4 stored on the laptop and a USB stick (not streamed from YouTube).
- Script every clip's meaning into the visuals and card text so the video makes sense muted; the presenter speaks over it live.
- Put a 10 s black "ready" frame at the start and hold the last frame at the end so the video can be paused without a jarring cut; test in the actual presentation software and projector at the venue.
- Do not trust autoplay; use the player/laptop locally and know the keyboard shortcut for pause.

### Gaps
- No source on muted looping playback in Keynote/PowerPoint/Slides for stage use, nor on caption font sizes for a projector.
- Silent-viewing statistics are 2016-era and publisher-reported; no current independent figure found.

## 8. Captions, export settings for YouTube, and thumbnail

### Takeaway
Export MP4/H.264/AAC at 1080p, 8 Mbps (30 fps) or 12 Mbps (60 fps), 48 kHz audio; upload a 1280x720 JPG/PNG thumbnail under 2 MB; burn in captions for the stage/social version and also upload a caption file (or rely on auto-captions edited by hand) for YouTube.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube upload settings: MP4, H.264, AAC-LC (or Opus), 48 kHz, stereo or 5.1; progressive scan; keep the recorded frame rate; SDR 1080p: 8 Mbps (24/25/30 fps), 12 Mbps (48/50/60 fps); 1440p: 16 / 24 Mbps; 2160p: 35-45 / 53-68 Mbps; no edit lists, moov atom at front (Fast Start); 16:9 is standard on desktop — [YouTube Help, Recommended upload encoding settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171)
- Thumbnail: 1280x720 recommended (minimum width 640 px), JPG/GIF/PNG, under 2 MB, 16:9 — [YouTube Help, Add video thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431)
- Smaller images are stretched and pixelate; a PNG at max quality can exceed 2 MB, so re-export as JPG — search summary of [TechSmith](https://www.techsmith.com/blog/youtube-thumbnail-sizes/) and [vidIQ (2026)](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-size-measurements/)
- Burned-in captions vs sidecar: see section 7.
- Screen Studio export: choose format, size, compression preset; up to 4K 60 fps — [Screen Studio](https://screen.studio/create/product-demo-videos)

### Inferences
- Record at 30 fps for UI demos (smaller file, matches the 8 Mbps row); upload 1080p even if you record 4K, unless text is tiny.
- Thumbnail: take the single most legible app frame (or a title card from the deck), 3-5 words in large type, high contrast, same palette as the deck; check it at small size. No source evaluated thumbnail design quality for demo videos.
- Captions: write them from the script rather than relying on auto-generation; keep lines to 1-2 rows and under about 42 characters per line (common subtitle convention; not verified here).

### Gaps
- No verified source on caption line-length or styling standards.
- No source on thumbnail design principles specific to B2B demos (only technical specs).

## 9. Pitfalls (too long, too fast, unreadable text, sped-up cursor, loud music)

### Takeaway
Sourced pitfalls: running too long or opening too slowly, showing every feature instead of one job, static screens, audio problems, and perfectionism delaying the recording. "Sped-up cursor" and "unreadable text" are supported only indirectly (cursor smoothing, text-size rules).

### Cited Findings
- Showing every feature instead of one job; too long; animation that hides the real UI — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/product-demo-video-for-startups)
- Opening too slowly; explaining instead of showing; waiting for perfection — [VibrantSnap](https://www.vibrantsnap.com/blog/solo-founder-guide-to-product-demos) and search summary of vendor blogs
- Clicking ahead before the page responds; not pausing after important changes — [MacMyths](https://macmyths.com/how-to-record-your-website-for-a-demo/)
- Hotspot/label copy over 60 characters cut completion by 12% (interactive demos) — [Arcade 2026](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks)
- Loud music over speech: keep it 18-25 dB under voice — [Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/fix-background-music-too-loud-video/)
- Decorative zooms with no visible result — [NemoVideo](https://www.nemovideo.com/blog/ai-video-workflows/screen-studio-review)
- On-screen text must stay long enough to be read (about 1 s per 2-3 words; read-twice rule) — [Biteable](https://biteable.com/blog/video-word-count/), [SSW](https://www.ssw.com.au/rules/post-production-do-you-give-enough-time-to-read-texts-in-your-videos)

### Inferences
- "Too fast" and "sped-up cursor" are really the same failure: the viewer cannot follow what was clicked. Remedy = slow real-time cursor, a pause after each result, zoom only to show a result, and only speed up waits.
- "Unreadable text": enlarge browser zoom to 125-150%, hide dense panels, crop in rather than shrink; test by viewing the export at phone size.

### Gaps
- No quantitative source on how fast is "too fast" for UI demos, and none that measures harm from a sped-up cursor specifically.

## Step-by-step plan for a two-person team over two days (synthesis; mostly inference, with sources above)

Day 1 (about 5-6 h)
1. Hour 1: Agree the one job to show; write 110-170 words of narration plus 4-5 card texts; time it aloud at about 140 wpm (Consensus, Stackmatix).
2. Hour 1-2: Build title cards as slides in the pitch deck master; export 1920x1080 PNGs (inference).
3. Hour 2-3: Prep the app: demo account, seed data, fresh Chrome profile, bookmarks hidden, Do Not Disturb, 16:9 fixed capture area, browser zoom up (Supademo, MacMyths).
4. Hour 3-5: Record each step as a separate clip with a slow cursor and a pause after each result; keep a take log (Tella, MacMyths).

Day 2 (about 5-6 h)
5. Assemble in Screen Studio / any editor: trim dead time, review auto-zooms, speed up waits only (Screen Studio, NemoVideo).
6. Record voiceover (own voice or AI) per clip; level to -16/-14 LUFS; add music at least 18 dB under voice or skip (Vidpros, YouTube normalization).
7. Add captions; export master (MP4 1080p H.264, 8 Mbps/30 fps, AAC 48 kHz) and a silent, burned-in-caption stage version (YouTube Help; AutoCaption).
8. Make the 1280x720 thumbnail; upload to YouTube (unlisted first) and test on phone and laptop; copy the stage version onto the laptop and a USB drive.
