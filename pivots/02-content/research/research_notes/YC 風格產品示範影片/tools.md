# Mac tools for the polished "auto-zoom" screen-recording look (pricing, free tiers, effects)

All pages accessed 2026-10-27 unless noted. "Official" = vendor's own page fetched this session. Anything marked UNVERIFIED comes only from third-party aggregators or conflicts with another source. Several fetches returned a page summary from a small model, not raw page text, so exact figures should be re-checked at the link before purchase.

## 1. Comparison of tools: current pricing, free-tier limits, watermark, student discounts, Mac support, effects

### Takeaway
Screen Studio (Mac-only, now subscription-only: $9/mo billed yearly or $29/mo) is the benchmark; its 40% student discount makes it about $65/yr, but Recordly/OpenScreen (free, MIT/AGPL) and Cap ($0 free tier, or $58 one-time Desktop license) cover most of the same look. Tella, Focusee and Rapidemo are paid-only or exclude Mac; Arcade, Supademo and Loom are not Screen Studio substitutes for effects.

- **Cap Studio Mode**: editor with backgrounds, zooms, trimming, captions, export — [GitHub README](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap); AI captions on every recording — [cap.so/docs](https://cap.so/docs). Docs page does NOT say zooms trigger automatically on clicks, nor mention cursor smoothing or hide/enlarge; padding and rounded corners not mentioned there.
- **Recordly**: zoom suggestions from cursor activity plus manual zoom regions; rendered cursor with size, smoothing, motion blur, click bounce, sway; wallpapers, gradients, custom images, padding, rounded corners, blur, drop shadows; webcam bubble; timeline trims/speed/annotations; MP4/GIF; macOS 14+; captions not mentioned — [GitHub](https://github.com/webadderall/Recordly).
- **OpenScreen fork**: auto or manual zoom, cursor customisation and motion blur, on-device captions with editable transcript, backgrounds, crop/trim/speed, annotations, webcam PiP, mic + system audio, MP4/GIF, headless CLI; macOS 13+ (notarised builds since 1.9.0) — [GitHub fork](https://github.com/getopenscreen/openscreen). - **FocuSee**: auto zoom on clicks and typing; cursor styles, click animations and sounds, fade during focus; AI background removal/replacement, rounded corners, shadows; AI subtitles in 50+ languages, SRT import; device frames not mentioned; screen-records iOS/Android via USB — [focusee.imobie.com](https://focusee.imobie.com/).

### Inferences
- For a two-person student team the cheapest credible Screen-Studio-like paths are, in order: Recordly or the OpenScreen fork ($0), Cap free ($0, personal use) or Cap Desktop ($58 one-time, if a startup demo counts as commercial use), then Screen Studio with the 40% student discount (roughly $65/yr if the annual price is $108, an inference from $9 x 12 and 40% off; Trupeer's "approximately $65" agrees but is a third-party). Both founders only need one licence if one person records and edits.
- Cap's free tier is explicitly "personal use only"; a YC-style startup demo is arguably commercial, so the free tier may technically not apply (my reading of the plan wording, not a legal conclusion).
- Rapidemo should be dropped for a Mac team (Windows-only per its own page). Loom and ScreenFlow do not give the auto-zoom look.
- Tella has no free plan, but the education offer (6 months free Pro, no card) could cover the whole project at $0 if both teammates have school emails; each qualifying person gets one free term per workspace.

## 2. Free or open-source options closest to Screen Studio's look

### Takeaway
Recordly (35.3k stars, AGPL-3.0, macOS 14+) and the community OpenScreen fork (5.0k stars, MIT, macOS 13+) both advertise Screen-Studio-style auto-zoom, smoothed cursor, motion blur, backgrounds and 4K-class export for free; Cap is the more polished product but its free tier is personal-use only. None has independently verified output-quality comparisons against Screen Studio.

### Cited Findings
- Recordly: 35.3k stars, AGPL-3.0; zoom suggestions from cursor activity, manual zooms; cursor overlay with smoothing, motion blur, click bounce; wallpapers, gradients, padding, rounded corners, blur, shadows; webcam bubble; MP4/GIF; requires macOS 14+; project says "Accepting PRs" — [GitHub](https://github.com/webadderall/Recordly)
- Recordly is described as a heavily modified fork of OpenScreen with a full cursor rendering pipeline; a May 2026 TheSweetBits review reports 13,000+ stars since March 2026 launch (this differs from the 35.3k seen on GitHub, so star counts have moved or sources disagree) — [AlternativeTo search result summary](https://alternativeto.net/software/recordly/?p=4), [TheSweetBits](https://thesweetbits.com/tools/recordly/)
- OpenScreen original: 39.9k stars, MIT, ARCHIVED ("no longer maintained"), author says "not production grade" — [GitHub](https://github.com/siddharthvaddem/openscreen)
- OpenScreen community fork getopenscreen/openscreen: 5.0k stars, 335 forks, MIT, "actively maintained"; signed/notarised macOS builds from 1.9.0; macOS 13+; Homebrew or .dmg; needs Screen Recording and Accessibility permissions — [GitHub fork](https://github.com/getopenscreen/openscreen)
- Cap: 23.9k stars, AGPLv3 (some MIT crates); runs on macOS and Windows; self-hosting via Docker; Studio Mode edits backgrounds, zooms, trim, captions — [GitHub](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap). Pricing page: free for personal use, 4K/60fps export on all plans — [cap.so/pricing](https://cap.so/pricing)
- Screenize: 617 stars, Apache-2.0, auto-zoom + click ripples + gradients; development paused (2026-05-22), not notarised — [GitHub](https://github.com/syi0808/screenize)
- Others named only in listings (not checked): ScreenArc, Screenity, ShipClip, OBS Studio — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/screen-story)

### Inferences
- Recordly looks like the closest free match on paper (cursor rendering pipeline, click bounce, motion blur) but is young (launched March 2026) so expect bugs; keep the raw recording and try 2 free tools on the same 90-second take before choosing.
- Archived OpenScreen (original) should be avoided in favour of the fork or Recordly.
- Star counts here are inflated relative to maturity (a 5k-star fork vs a 39.9k-star archived original); do not treat stars as quality.

### Gaps
- No Reddit/X first-hand comparisons of export quality found (search returned only directory listings and vendor-style comparison pages).
- Last-commit dates and latest release tags not shown by the fetched GitHub pages (Releases section empty in the fetch tool), so "last release" for Cap, Recordly, OpenScreen fork and Screenize is unknown.
- Whether Cap's free tier applies a watermark: not stated.

## 3. Keynote or Canva for cheap title cards

### Takeaway
Yes. Keynote (free on Mac) can export a title-card slide to a movie file, and Canva Free can export MP4 without a watermark provided only free-licence elements are used. Add the cards in the final editor (iMovie/CapCut/DaVinci Resolve, none researched here) or just record them as stills. Check Canva's free resolution before relying on it.

### Cited Findings
- Keynote: File > Export To > Movie, with 1080p and H.264 or ProRes choices described in Elephorm guides (French) — [Elephorm](https://www.elephorm.com/formation/maitriser-keynote-2026/exporter-un-diaporama-keynote-en-video-guide-etape-par-etape)
- A MacScripter thread describes a script exporting Keynote to H.264 or ProRes 4444 at 1920x1080, with transparency as the goal — [MacScripter](https://macscripter.net/viewtopic.php?id=47383)
- Canva Free users can download MP4 without a watermark if the design uses only free elements, images, footage and music; Pro-tagged (crown) content gets a watermark unless the user buys a $1 Pro Content License or Canva Pro — [Canva help (from search result)](https://www.canva.com/en_gb/help/download-video-as-separate-files/)
- Same Canva help snippet lists free limits including MP4 up to 30 minutes and GIFs up to 1 minute (page text was partly garbled in the search result) — [Canva help](https://www.canva.com/en_gb/help/download-video-as-separate-files/); a third-party blog says 5 minutes and 1080p cap, so sources conflict — [Style Factory Productions](https://www.stylefactoryproductions.com/blog/video-canva-pro-vs-free)
- Canva's brand-kit help says uploaded 4K video is stored at 1080p (unrelated to export resolution) — [Canva help](https://www.canva.com/help/brand-videos/)
- Among recorders, Tella and ScreenFlow have text overlays/animations in their editors (ScreenFlow official; Tella not verified), so cards may not need a separate tool in those — [ScreenFlow](https://www.telestream.net/screenflow/overview.htm)

### Inferences
- A 90-second video needs maybe two or three static cards (title, tagline/CTA); a Keynote or Canva Free slide exported as a still PNG or 3-5 second clip is enough and costs $0.
- Keynote's native movie export is a QuickTime .mov (my understanding, not stated by the fetched sources); YouTube accepts .mov, so conversion is probably unnecessary.

### Gaps
- Canva Free export resolution (1080p vs 4K) and whether students get Pro: not confirmed on an official page.
- Whether Recordly/Cap/OpenScreen offer title cards or text overlays natively: Recordly and OpenScreen list annotations, but title-card support is not stated.
- The final-assembly editor (iMovie, DaVinci Resolve, CapCut) was not researched.

## 4. Interactive demos (Arcade, Supademo) vs video, and which suit a 90-second YouTube upload

### Takeaway
Arcade and Supademo are interactive-demo tools whose free tiers are small (Arcade Free is capped at 30-second videos with a watermark; Supademo Free allows 5 demos and 50 recordings) and they pivoted toward AI/sales-onboarding use; for a 90-second YouTube upload, a video recorder (Screen Studio, Cap, Recordly, FocuSee, Tella) is the better fit.

### Cited Findings
- Arcade's pricing page now describes "AI Video" plans: Free = 30-second max, 1080p, watermark; Pro = 5-minute videos, 4K, no watermark, $49/mo annual — [arcade.software/pricing](https://www.arcade.software/pricing)
- Arcade homepage headlines AI video for marketing teams; Interactive Demos listed as a separate product; has Chrome extension — [arcade.software](https://www.arcade.software)
- Third-party (Trainn, 2026-09-07): Arcade gives a clickable demo plus MP4/GIF export; Supademo gives clickable walkthroughs; Supademo free plan shows branding, Scale is watermark-free; Arcade free = 1 demo + 1 video, 200 AI credits (conflicts with the official 250 credits above, so the article is dated or inexact) — [Trainn](https://trainn.co/blog/arcade-vs-supademo/)
- Supademo official page: free plan includes 5 guided interactive demos, 50 video recordings (4K), sharing as link, embed, video or PDF; no student discount or watermark statement on the page — [supademo.com/pricing](https://supademo.com/pricing)
- Supademo's own comparison and other competitor pages report Arcade's free tier as 1-3 demos with watermark; sources disagree — [Guidejar, July 2026](https://www.guidejar.com/compare/arcade-vs-supademo)
- Loom: Starter plan 5-minute recordings at 720p, so a 90-second take fits, but no auto-zoom and quality is capped at 720p on free — [loom.com/pricing](https://www.loom.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- Interactive demos shine as clickable embeds on a landing page or in outreach; they are not what makes the "YC-style polished video" look. A 90-second YouTube upload needs a video file: use a recorder with auto-zoom and export MP4 at 1080p or 4K.
- Arcade Free's 30-second ceiling (official) rules it out for a 90-second video without paying $49/mo.
- Supademo Free's 50 video recordings (4K) are interesting but its effects (zoom, cursor smoothing) are not verified.

### Gaps
- Supademo Free watermark on MP4/video export: official page silent; third-party claims conflict.
- Whether Arcade/Supademo have a student or startup discount: Arcade page lists none; Supademo not stated.
- Arcade's interactive-demo plan (separate from the AI Video pricing) could not be verified; the fetched page covered AI video plans only.
- Reddit/X discussion on which suits YouTube demos: not found in this research.
