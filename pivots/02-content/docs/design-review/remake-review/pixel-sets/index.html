Remake · design review, round 2 · 4 Nov 2026

Three pixel directions, from a hint to a whole language.

A fresh start, not a reskin of Broadsheet, Control Room or Signal. Each concept takes pixels at a different strength, and in each one the pixels stand for something Remake actually does: count videos, show how far a video beat its usual views, or render a plan as it moves forward. All 22 screens are covered in every concept, with the content, fields and actions from the app (lib/strings and the live app at :5192). Only six colours are used: navy, electric blue, mustard, beige, black and white.

A · subtle

Unit

One pixel, one thing that counts.

A clean, modern Geist interface on white and beige. A pixel only appears when Remake is counting something real: one square per video the client pays for this month, per second of a shot, per account posting a trend, per "usual" view count (Best to copy draws 267 of them). Mustard marks the one thing waiting for you. Motion is a 12-frames-per-second "frame step", like scrubbing a video.

Type
Geist for everything you read, Geist Mono for numbers and handles, Silkscreen only on small counts. Geist keeps it calm and current; the bitmap face is a seasoning.
Palette
White screens, beige panels and sidebar, navy text, blue for done and for primary actions, mustard only for "needs you", black almost unused.

B · moderate

Dither

A print shop for video ideas.

Pixel type for headings, buttons and navigation, pixel-art covers and a pixel kitchen on the landing page, buttons that press down 3 pixels. No tints exist: every shade is an ordered dither of two palette colours. Dither density means one thing everywhere: heat, or how far a video beat its usual views, on a 16-step scale shared by cover borders, the search contact sheet and Best to copy. Video slots are film frames; the month is a reel. Screens change with a Bayer-order dissolve.

Type
Pixelify Sans (headings, buttons, nav) with Atkinson Hyperlegible (reading) and its Mono (numbers). Pixelify brings the character; Atkinson was designed for legibility, which matters with a grade 5 to 7 copy guide.
Palette
Beige paper, white cards with black 2 px borders and hard navy shadows, blue for actions and heat, mustard dither for "waiting for you", navy for headings.

C · expressive

Render

Copy, then rebuild: resolution is progress.

Remake rebuilds a video that already worked, so every plan is a frame that renders as it moves on: idea 3 × 5 pixels, hook picked 4 × 8, shot list sent 6 × 11, approved 9 × 16, filmed 18 × 32. Home shows the month as 12 frames at their resolutions; Plans cards carry a five-step render bar. "267 times usual" is a tower of stacked pixels, an approval collapses into one mustard pixel that flies into the month, headline words assemble from scattered pixels, and links on the planning board are stepped pixel lines.

Type
Jersey 10 (display), Tiny5 (data labels), Bricolage Grotesque (reading). Jersey's big visible pixels carry the identity at size; Bricolage has enough warmth to read well on navy.
Palette
Navy ground with a blue pixel grid, mustard for lit pixels and primary actions, white text; tables, forms and review cards switch to beige paper with navy text.

My recommendation

Build the product on Dither, and borrow Render's one big idea: plans that render sharper as they move forward.

  1. Dither as the system. It is the only one of the three where the pixels are part of every component (buttons, type, covers, borders, states) and still read as a polished tool. It stays light like today's app, Atkinson keeps long text easy to read, and "heat" gives the main ranking rule (views compared with usual) one visual meaning across Results, Research, Searching and Landing without a × score.
  2. Render's resolution metaphor on Plans and Home. A plan's frame going from 3 × 5 to 18 × 32 is the most original idea in this round and the most specific to Remake (copy, then rebuild). It drops into Dither's film frames easily, so the November reel would show real progress instead of three states.
  3. Render for the landing page and the approval moment. The assembling headline, the feed with three sharp winners and the approval pixel flying into the month are marketing-grade; keep them for the landing page and the review swipe.
  4. Unit as the fallback if Dither tests as too loud with agency owners: it keeps the same counting logic and costs least to build from today's tokens.

Decide before building. Render is dark by default; today the app follows the system theme and opens light. Render would need a light variant or a change to that decision in docs/decision-log.md. All three keep the sidebar navigation from the current app.

Chinese (繁體中文). Pixelify Sans, Jersey 10, Tiny5 and Silkscreen have no Traditional Chinese glyphs, so in the zh-Hant setting headings fall back to a system font. Dither and Render would need a CJK fallback plan (for example DotGothic16 for Japanese kanji overlap, or keep CJK in a plain face).
