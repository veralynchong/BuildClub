Summary

The words are mostly plain already. There’s no “leverage”, “unlock” or “seamless”, and most errors say what happened and what to do next. What makes it read as machine-written is the structure: small uppercase labels above headings, numbered tabs, a sub-line under every heading that repeats it, and build notes left on screen. The second problem is naming. One thing can have up to five names across screens, and the landing page promises things the app contradicts.

Top 10 problems

  1. Labels stacked above headings on almost every screen. “EVERGREEN FOODS · RESEARCH” over “Research”, “EVERGREEN FOODS · PLANS” over “Plans”, “CHANNEL” over “Analyse a channel”, and “FIND IDEAS” over “Compare videos”, which isn’t even under Find ideas. The sidebar already says where you are.
  2. Numbers that mean nothing. “01. RECENT SEARCHES” and “02. SAVED TO RESEARCH” sit above headings with the same words. Where to start numbers its rows 01 to 08, the saved table has a “No.” column, and results tabs read “01. Best to copy”.
  3. Build notes and demo scaffolding on screen. “Needs a decision before we build this.” and “Would need: access to…” appear three times on each home page. “Samples written from this idea for the demo”, “Match · mock”, and on the public landing page, “Quote from an agency goes here (waiting for their OK)”.
  4. The landing page promises things the app contradicts. “We don’t write your content” and the FAQ answer “Does it write scripts? No.” sit next to an app with “Write the script”, “Suggested hooks” and “AI voice”. The landing page says “TikTok today, Instagram coming soon”, but the app has Instagram switched on by default and Compare takes five platforms. Also, “No sign-up” on the landing page, then “Register free” on the login page.
  5. One thing, many names. Home, Dashboard and Client dashboard. Brief, About this client and Client profile. Plans and Shortlist. Reference, video to copy and original. Shot list, Shot checklist, Script and storyboard, and planning board. Post and Publish. Video and cut. Manager and approver. “Brief” also means the notes you copy for the editor.
  6. Developer and vendor words. Apify, yt-dlp, fal, whisper.cpp, “npm run api”, “raise … in .env and restart the API”, node, provider, mock, cached, scraping quota.
  7. Analyst words around the numbers. Baseline, median, multiple, cohort, hit rate, “Evidence: limited”. Agencies say it plainly: in the Mercif interview it was “a video with 1M+ views from an account with about 1k followers”.
  8. A sub-line under every heading that repeats it. Research, Plans, Brief, Find ideas, Compare, Channel and Settings all open with one or two sentences that restate the heading. Several are three-part lists (“suggest searches, check what they can film, and track the monthly target”).
  9. Steps written out as prose. ‘Set its status to “★ Picked” in Research to plan it’, “Drop it on a link between two shots”, “Press + on a link”. The UI should make these obvious, so the text doesn’t have to.
  10. Small mismatches that add up. “Could not” (20 strings) next to “Couldn’t”, “Retry” and “Try again”, “Sign out” and “Log in”, “{x}×” and “{x} times”, “Take back” and “Take it back”, plus a few cute lines (“Steal this.”, “Your plans, soon.”, “decoded”).

Voice and tone

Remake should sound like a sharp account manager: short, specific, and on the agency’s side. These rules come from how the agencies we interviewed talk (the Mercif interview notes use “research”, “shortlist”, “recreate”, “approve” and “reference video”) and from the landing-page writing rules already agreed (grade 5–7 reading level, one idea per sentence).

Name it, then stop

A heading names the thing. Add a line under it only if it gives a fact the heading doesn’t.

Do
Research
Don’t
Research / Reference videos worth copying, grouped by the videos the client is paying for. Plan the best one in each slot.

No labels over headings

The sidebar and the back link show where you are. Group labels are sentence case, never mono uppercase.

Do
Plans
Don’t
EVERGREEN FOODS · PLANS / Plans

Use the agency’s words

Client, reference, film, approve, send back, usual views. Keep model names and prompts inside Canvas.

Do
52× their usual views
Don’t
Views ÷ baseline median = 52 · Evidence: limited

One name for each thing

Use the glossary below. If two screens use different words, someone will think they’re two features.

Do
Brief, on every screen
Don’t
Brief / About this client / Client profile

Errors: what happened, then what to do

Start with “Couldn’t”. End with the next step. Never name our vendors or tools.

Do
Couldn’t load the video. Try again.
Don’t
The provider didn’t answer. It may have charged.

Don’t ship notes to ourselves

If something isn’t built, hide it. Demo state goes in one place (the Sample data pill), not on every card.

Do
(no card)
Don’t
Needs a decision before we build this.

Plain beats clever

No slang, no wordplay, no cute fragments. Agencies told us AI-sounding copy is a turn-off.

Do
What to copy
Don’t
Steal this.

Traditional Chinese

The Traditional Chinese strings mirror the English closely, so every flagged English key needs the same change in zhHant. Examples: “01. 最近的搜尋” and “02. 已存到研究” keep the numbered labels, “{client} · 研究” keeps the label above the heading, “開發前需要先做決定。” is the same build note, “可以偷學。” is “Steal this.”, and the YouTube source line still names yt-dlp. “儀表板” and “客戶儀表板” carry the Dashboard naming split. Change the English first, then update the matching key.

Also: the landing page, login, shared review link and How we calculate this pages are hardcoded in English and have no Chinese at all. The language setting says “The language of menus and screens”, so a Chinese user lands on English pages there.
